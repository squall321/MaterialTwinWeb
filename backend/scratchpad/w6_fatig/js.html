    The size of defects in castings affects mechanical properties such as

    fatigue
    strength. These properties are the most important properties used for designing aluminum alloy
    casting
    components, and usually increase with decreasing size of internal defects. Large internal defects mainly form due to the volumetric shrinkage of melt at the last solidified position. In this study, we therefore examined the possibility of generating microbubbles in aluminum alloy melts for dispersing internal defects in castings.

    Ultrasonic treatment was used for generating nitrogen or argon microbubbles in the melt and was found to be suitable for forming fine gas porosities dispersed in castings.
